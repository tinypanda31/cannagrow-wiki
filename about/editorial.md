# Editorial Guidelines

These guidelines define the editorial standards for all content published on CannaGrow. Every contributor and editor is expected to be familiar with and follow these standards.

**✅ Scope** These guidelines apply to **all content** on CannaGrow, including articles, strain profiles, data tables, images, and page descriptions. Content that does not meet these standards will be returned for revision or removed.


---

## 1. Neutral Point of View (NPOV)

All CannaGrow content must be written from a **neutral point of view**. This is a foundational principle.

### What NPOV Means

- Present information **fairly and without bias**, representing significant viewpoints proportionately
- Describe cannabis-related topics **educationally**, without advocating for or against use
- Acknowledge **controversies and uncertainties** where they exist
- Attribute **opinions and claims** to their sources, not to CannaGrow as an authority
- Avoid **loaded language** that implies judgment (e.g., "dangerous drug," "miracle cure," "harmless herb")

### Examples

| Not Acceptable | Acceptable |
|---------------|------------|
| "Cannabis is a safe, natural medicine with no side effects." | "Some clinical studies have found cannabis effective for certain conditions with manageable side effects, though risks exist and vary by individual." |
| "Cannabis prohibition was an unjust war on personal freedom." | "Cannabis prohibition has been criticized by some as an infringement on personal liberty and defended by others as a public health measure." |
| "This strain will cure your anxiety." | "Some users report reduced anxiety with this cultivar; clinical evidence is limited and results vary." |

### Handling Controversial Topics

When covering topics where significant disagreement exists (e.g., medical efficacy for specific conditions, gateway theory, economic impacts of legalization):

1. **Identify the major viewpoints** and their proponents
2. **Present the evidence** each viewpoint relies on
3. **Note the weight of evidence** (e.g., "A 2023 systematic review of 47 studies found..." vs. "A single 2019 study suggested...")
4. **Avoid false balance** — do not give fringe views equal weight to well-supported consensus positions

## 2. Evidence-Based Claims

All factual claims must be **verifiable** through reliable sources.

### Evidence Hierarchy

Sources are evaluated in the following order of reliability (highest to lowest):

| Tier | Source Type | When to Use |
|------|------------|-------------|
| **1** | Peer-reviewed meta-analyses and systematic reviews | Preferred for all scientific and medical claims |
| **2** | Peer-reviewed individual studies | Acceptable when meta-analyses are not available |
| **3** | Government and institutional reports | Acceptable for legal, regulatory, and statistical data |
| **4** | Academic books and textbooks | Acceptable for foundational and historical information |
| **5** | Reputable journalism with expert citations | Acceptable for current events and recent legal changes |
| **6** | Industry reports and expert testimony | Use with caution; note potential conflicts of interest |
| **7** | Anecdotal reports and community knowledge | Only for documenting cultural practices; not for scientific claims |

### Citation Requirements

- **Every factual claim** that is not common knowledge must be cited
- **Statistical data** must include the source, date, and sample size (if applicable)
- **Conflicting findings** must be acknowledged, not omitted
- **Outdated sources** should be flagged; prefer the most recent reliable data available
- **Citation format**: Use inline numbered citations with links to sources where available

### What Not to Cite

| Source | Reason |
|--------|--------|
| Social media posts | Not verifiable or authoritative |
| Anonymous forum posts | No accountability or expertise verification |
| Marketing materials from cannabis companies | Inherent conflict of interest |
| Unpublished manuscripts | Not peer-reviewed or publicly verifiable |
| Personal blogs (without expertise) | No editorial oversight or credentialing |

## 3. Tone and Style

### General Tone

- **Educational and informative** — write as if explaining to a university student
- **Objective and measured** — avoid enthusiasm, skepticism, or advocacy in your language
- **Accessible** — define technical terms on first use; link to the [Glossary](/glossary) where appropriate
- **Precise** — use specific terminology correctly; do not conflate related but distinct concepts

### Language Standards

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Language** | English (US spelling preferred) |
| **Voice** | Prefer active voice where clarity improves; passive voice is acceptable where standard in scientific writing |
| **Tense** | Use present tense for established facts, past tense for historical events, and conditional for uncertain claims |
| **Abbreviations** | Spell out on first use: "Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)" |
| **Species names** | *Cannabis sativa* (italicized, lowercase species epithet) on first mention; "cannabis" (lowercase, non-italic) thereafter |
| **Measurements** | Use metric units primarily; imperial in parentheses where relevant for US audiences |

### Prohibited Language

Do not use language that:

- Promotes or discourages cannabis use
- Makes definitive medical claims without strong clinical evidence
- Uses slang in article body text (slang terms may appear in the [Glossary](/glossary) with attribution)
- Stereotypes or stigmatizes cannabis users, patients, or opponents
- Implies legality or illegality without jurisdictional context

## 4. Formatting Standards

### Markdown Structure

All articles use standard Markdown formatting:

```markdown
---
title: Page Title
description: One-sentence summary
tags:
  - relevant-tag
---

# Page Title

Brief introduction paragraph.

## Section Heading

Content paragraph with inline citations^[1].

### Subsection Heading

More content.

## See Also

- [Related Page](/path)
- [Another Related Page](/path)

## References

1. [Author, Title, Journal, Year, DOI/URL](link)
```

### Headings

- Use `#` for the page title (H1) — only one per page
- Use `##` for major sections (H2)
- Use `###` for subsections (H3)
- Do not skip heading levels (e.g., H2 to H4)

### Lists

- Use **ordered lists** (1., 2., 3.) for sequences and ranked items
- Use **unordered lists** (- or *) for non-sequential items
- Keep list items parallel in structure (same grammatical form)

### Tables

Tables should be used for structured data that benefits from side-by-side comparison:

| Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Data | Data | Data |
| Data | Data | Data |

- Always include a **header row**
- Ensure column headers are **clear and descriptive**
- Add a **caption or context paragraph** before or after complex tables

### Admonitions

Use Wiki.js admonition blocks for special content types:

| Block | Syntax | Use Case |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Info | `**ℹ️ Info** title + content | Supplementary context, tips, cross-references |
| Warning | `**⚠️ Warning** title + content | Legal disclaimers, safety concerns, important caveats |
| Danger | `**🚨 Danger** title + content | Immediate safety risks (contamination, overconsumption, etc.) |
| Tip | `**💡 Tip** title + content | Practical suggestions for readers |

### Internal Links

- Use internal links liberally to connect related content
- Format: `[Page Title](/path/to/page)`
- Link on the **first mention** of a term that has its own page
- Do not over-link the same term multiple times in a single section
- Always use **absolute paths** starting with `/`

### External Links

- Use external links for **citations and references**, not for general navigation
- External links in body text should be used when a concept is too detailed for CannaGrow but relevant as a resource
- Format: `[Source Name](https://...)`

## 5. Image Guidelines

### When to Include Images

- Plant morphology diagrams and photos
- Equipment and setup illustrations
- Chemical structure diagrams for cannabinoids
- Historical photographs and documents
- Maps (for cultivation regions, legal status, historical distribution)

### Image Requirements

| Requirement | Standard |
|-------------|----------|
| **Quality** | Minimum 800px on the longest side; clear and in-focus |
| **Format** | WebP or JPEG for photos; SVG for diagrams; PNG for screenshots |
| **Attribution** | Every image must include source, author, and license |
| **Licensing** | Prefer CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, or public domain. No unlicensed images. |
| **Alt text** | Descriptive alt text is required for all images |
| **Captions** | Captions should explain what the image shows and why it is relevant |

### Image Placeholder Format

When an image is needed but not yet available, insert a placeholder:

```markdown
> *[Image placeholder: Description of what the image should show. Suggested source or creation method.]*
```

Example:
> *[Image placeholder: Diagram of the cannabis plant showing key morphological features — leaf shape, flowering structure, and trichome locations. Suggested: Original SVG diagram or CC-licensed botanical illustration.]*

## 6. Strain Profile Standards

Strain profiles in the [Strains](/strains) section must follow a standardized format:

### Required Fields

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Strain Name** | Common cultivar name |
| **Synonyms** | Known alternate names |
| **Genetics** | Parent cultivars and breeding lineage (if known) |
| **Type** | Indica-dominant, Sativa-dominant, Hybrid, or Balanced |
| **THC Range** | Typical range with source attribution |
| **CBD Range** | Typical range with source attribution |
| **Dominant Terpenes** | Top 3-5 terpenes with approximate percentages |
| **Flavor/Aroma** | Commonly reported sensory descriptors |
| **Reported Effects** | Commonly reported effects (not medical claims) |
| **Growing Notes** | Key cultivation considerations (if applicable) |
| **Origin/History** | Breeder, region of origin, year of introduction |

### Strain Profile Template

```markdown
---
title: Strain Name
type: strain
tags:
  - indica-dominant
  - hybrid
  - strain-profile
---

# Strain Name

Brief introduction covering genetics and origin.

## Overview

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Type | ... |
| THC | ... |
| CBD | ... |
| Genetics | ... |

## Cannabinoid Profile

Description of cannabinoid content with sourcing.

## Terpene Profile

Description of dominant terpenes.

## Reported Effects

Summary of commonly reported effects (subjective, with caveats).

## Cultivation

Growing notes if applicable.

## History

Origin and breeding history.

## See Also

- [Related Strain](/strains/strain-name)
```

**✅ Strain Data Limitations** Cannabinoid and terpene percentages vary by grower, harvest, and testing methodology. Profile data should represent **ranges** from multiple sources, not single test results. Always note your sources.

## 7. Review Process

### How to Contribute Edits via GitHub

Wiki.js 2.x does not handle in-platform user edits reliably. **All edits to CannaGrow are made through the GitHub repository**, and then synced to the live wiki via Git storage.

**To submit an edit, new page, or correction:**

1. Go to the [CannaGrow GitHub repository](https://github.com/tinypanda31/cannagrow-wiki/)
2. **Fork the repo** (or request collaborator access if you are a regular contributor)
3. Make your changes to the relevant `.md` file, or create a new one
4. Commit your changes with a clear message explaining what you changed and why
5. Open a **Pull Request** to the `main` branch
6. An editor will review your changes before they are merged and synced to the live wiki

### How Edits Are Reviewed

1. **Submission** — A contributor opens a Pull Request on GitHub with their proposed changes
2. **Initial check** — An editor verifies the submission meets basic formatting and NPOV requirements
3. **Substantive review** — An editor with relevant expertise evaluates:
   - Accuracy of factual claims
   - Quality and sufficiency of citations
   - Completeness of coverage
   - Adherence to tone and style guidelines
   - Appropriateness of images and data
4. **Feedback** — The editor provides specific feedback via Pull Request comments
5. **Revision** — The contributor addresses feedback and updates the PR
6. **Merge & Sync** — Once approved, the PR is merged and Wiki.js pulls the updated content from GitHub

### Review Criteria Checklist

- [ ] Neutral point of view maintained throughout
- [ ] All factual claims are cited to reliable sources
- [ ] Evidence hierarchy respected (tier 1 sources preferred)
- [ ] Tone is educational and objective
- [ ] Formatting follows Markdown standards
- [ ] Internal links to relevant CannaGrow pages included
- [ ] Disclaimer sections present where required (medical, legal)
- [ ] Images properly attributed and licensed
- [ ] Strain profiles (if applicable) follow template
- [ ] No promotion of illegal activity
- [ ] Uncertainty and conflicting evidence acknowledged where relevant

### Expedited Review

Minor edits (typo corrections, formatting fixes, broken link repairs, citation additions) may be approved without full substantive review at the discretion of the reviewing editor.

## 8. Prohibited Content

The following content is not permitted on CannaGrow:

| Content Type | Reason |
|-------------|--------|
| Unverified medical claims presented as fact | Misinformation risk |
| Personal anecdotes presented as evidence | Not verifiable or generalizable |
| Defamatory content about individuals or organizations | Legal and ethical liability |
| Copyrighted material without permission or license | Copyright infringement |
| Advertising or promotional content for cannabis businesses | Conflict of interest and commercial bias |

---

## 9. Updates and Maintenance

### Keeping Content Current

- **Legal pages** must be reviewed at least every 6 months due to the pace of legal change
- **Scientific pages** should be reviewed when significant new research is published
- **Strain profiles** should be updated when new testing data becomes available
- Contributors are encouraged to flag pages they believe need updating

### Page History

All edits are tracked in page history. Major revisions should include an edit summary explaining what was changed and why.

---

## 10. Questions and Clarifications

If you are uncertain whether your content meets these standards:

1. Review this document carefully
2. Check comparable published pages for examples
3. Open a Discussion on the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/tinypanda31/cannagrow-wiki/) to ask questions
4. When in doubt, err on the side of caution — cite more, claim less

**✅ Remember** The goal of these guidelines is not to restrict expression but to ensure that every reader who visits CannaGrow finds information they can trust. Quality standards protect both the reader and the credibility of the project.
