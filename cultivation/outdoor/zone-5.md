## Zone 5 Overview

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Temperature Range** | −30°F to −20°F (−34°C to −29°C) average extreme minimum |
| **Frost-Free Days** | ~150-170 days |
| **Last Frost** | Late April to late May |
| **First Frost** | Late September to late October |
| **Growing Season** | Solid for autos and short-to-mid season photoperiods |
| **Recommended Method** | Both autoflowers and photoperiods viable outdoors |
| **Best Genetics** | Autoflowering; indica-dominant and balanced hybrids (8-10 week flower) |
| **Regions** | Midwest US (Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Iowa, Nebraska), Northeast US (Pennsylvania, New York, New Jersey), Southern Ontario, Pacific Northwest lowlands, parts of Central Europe |

**ℹ️ Zone 5 Reality:** With 150-170 frost-free days, Zone 5 is where outdoor cannabis cultivation becomes reliably productive. Autoflowers perform well with room for multiple runs. Photoperiod plants have enough time to build substantial vegetative structure before flowering triggers in late summer. This is the northern edge of comfortable outdoor growing.

## The Zone 5 Growing Strategy

### Autoflower Path
- Start indoors mid-April or direct sow late May
- Harvest by mid-August (first run) to late September (second run)
- Two full cycles per season are achievable

### Photoperiod Path
- Start indoors mid-March to early April
- Transplant outdoors mid-to-late May
- Natural light triggers flowering late August
- Harvest mid-to-late October

### Month-by-Month Calendar

| Month | Autoflower Path | Photoperiod Path |
|-------|----------------|------------------|
| **March** | — | Start seeds indoors (mid-March) |
| **April** | Start seeds indoors or direct sow late month (with protection) | Indoor veg; begin LST training |
| **May** | Transplant or direct sow after last frost | Harden off; transplant outdoors (mid-late May) |
| **June** | Peak growth and early flower (first run) | Outdoor vegetative growth; training |
| **July** | Harvest first run; plant second autos | Peak vegetative; final training by mid-July |
| **August** | Second run flowering | Flowering begins; stretch phase |
| **September** | Harvest second run | Peak bud development |
| **October** | Garden cleanup | Harvest mid-to-late October |

| Item | Purpose | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Frost blankets** | May and October protection | Keep through first week of June |
| **Indoor seed-starting light** | 6-8 week head start for photoperiods | LED panel or T5 |

## Soil Preparation

Zone 5 soils include the rich loams of the Midwest, the clay soils of the Northeast, and the volcanic soils of the Pacific Northwest.

### In-Ground Amendment (per plant)

| Amendment | Amount | Purpose |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| **Compost** | 3-4 gallons | Organic matter and nutrients |
| **Worm castings** | 1-2 gallons | Available nutrients and biology |
| **Perlite** | 1 gallon | Drainage (essential for clay soils) |
| **Bone meal** | 3 tbsp | Phosphorus |
| **Kelp meal** | 2 tbsp | Micronutrients |
| **Mycorrhizal inoculant** | Per label | Root symbiosis |

## Growing Calendar — Photoperiod Path

### March: Germination
- Start seeds indoors under LED (18/6)
- Transplant to 1-gallon pots at 2-3 true leaves

### April: Indoor Vegetative
- Increase light intensity to 400-600 PPFD
- Begin LST at 4-5 nodes
- Light feeding at 25% strength

### May: Transplant
- Harden off 7-10 days
- Transplant after last frost into amended soil
- Water in well; begin bi-weekly organic feeding

### June-July: Outdoor Vegetative
- Complete training (topping, LST, ScrOG)
- Feed bi-weekly; monitor for nutrient demand
- Install trellis netting

### August: Flowering Transition
- Natural day length triggers flowering late August
- Switch to bloom nutrients (higher P, K)
- Defoliate large fan leaves blocking bud sites

### September: Flowering
- Buds fatten rapidly
- Monitor for mold in humid conditions
- Support heavy branches

### October: Harvest
- Trichome check mid-October
- Harvest when 70-80% cloudy
- Dry indoors (60-70°F, 45-55% RH)

## Pest and Disease Management

| Issue | Risk | Prevention |
|-------|------|------------|
| **Spider mites** | Moderate-High (hot, dry summers) | Predatory mites; neem oil (veg only) |
| **Powdery mildew** | Moderate | Airflow; avoid overhead watering |
| **Bud rot** | Moderate (humid falls) | Shake after rain; remove affected buds |
| **Aphids** | Moderate | Ladybugs; companion planting (marigolds) |
| **Caterpillars** | Moderate (August-September) | Bt spray every 2 weeks |

See [Pests Diseases](/cultivation/pests-diseases).

## Common Zone 5 Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| **Starting photoperiods too late (May)** | Plants too small at flower trigger | Start indoors by mid-March |
| **Choosing 12+ week sativas** | Won't finish before frost | Stick to 8-10 week varieties |
| **Ignoring spider mites in July** | Rapid population explosion in heat | Scout weekly; treat early |
| **Not supporting branches in September** | Heavy colas snap in wind | Install trellis by July |
