# Ice-Water Hash

Ice-water hash -- commonly known as bubble hash -- is a solventless cannabis concentrate produced by agitating frozen plant material in ice-cold water, then filtering the released trichomes through a series of mesh bags. The name "bubble hash" derives from the characteristic bubbling and popping sound high-quality hash makes when vaporized on a hot surface, indicating clean combustion of resinous trichome heads.

![Ice-water hash bubble bags setup](/uploads/images/extraction/ice-water-hash.jpg)

This method produces some of the cleanest, most flavorful concentrates achievable without solvents, preserving the full spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes present in the source material.

## How Ice-Water Extraction Works

The process relies on three physical principles:

1. **Cold embrittlement** -- Freezing temperatures cause trichome stalks to become brittle and snap off the plant surface
2. **Agitation** -- Physical movement dislodges trichome heads from plant material
3. **Density separation** -- Trichomes are denser than water and sink, while lighter plant matter floats
4. **Filtration** -- Mesh bags of decreasing micron rating separate trichomes by size

Ice is the critical component: it lowers the water temperature sufficiently to make trichomes brittle and facilitates clean separation. Without adequate ice, trichomes may tear rather than snap, producing lower-quality hash with more contamination.

A typical bubble bag set includes the following bags (listed from coarsest to finest):

| Micron | Purpose | Hash Quality |

| **190** | First filtration -- removes large debris | Low grade; some usable material |

| **120** | Mid-range trichomes | Good to very good quality |
| **90** | Mid-range trichomes | Very good quality |

**ℹ️ The 73 and 45 micron bags typically produce the highest quality hash. Trichome heads in this size range are most likely to be intact and pure, yielding full-melt hash suitable for dabbing.**

### Optional but Recommended

- **Washing machine** -- Dedicated front-loading washing machine (never used for food) provides consistent agitation
- **Freeze dryer** -- Professional-grade drying preserves quality far better than air drying

- **Thermometer** -- Monitor water temperature (ideal: 32-40 degrees F / 0-4 degrees C)

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Prepare and Freeze Material

Properly frozen starting material is essential for quality ice-water hash.

**Using fresh frozen material:**
1. Harvest cannabis plants or branches
2. Trim buds from stems (can include sugar leaves)
3. Place material in sealed bags or containers immediately
4. Freeze at 0 degrees F (-18 degrees C) or colder for at least 24 hours
5. Keep frozen until ready to wash

**Using dried and cured material:**
1. Ensure material is fully dried (60-65% relative humidity)
2. Freeze for at least 24-48 hours before washing
3. Note: cured material typically produces slightly lower yields than fresh frozen tip
Fresh frozen material -- harvested and immediately frozen without drying or curing -- preserves the maximum terpene profile and is the starting material for [Live Resin](/extraction/live-resin) and [Live Rosin](/extraction/live-rosin).
:::

### Step 2: Set Up the Bubble Bags

1. Place the collection vessel on a stable, level surface
2. Nest all bags inside the vessel, ordered from largest micron (top) to smallest micron (bottom)
3. Fold the top edges of each bag over the rim of the vessel to create a stable stack
4. Ensure all bag labels (micron ratings) are visible for identification

### Step 3: Add Ice, Water, and Material

1. Add a layer of ice to the bottom of the innermost (220 micron) bag
2. Add the frozen cannabis material on top of the ice
3. Cover with more ice -- a good ratio is approximately 1 part material to 2-3 parts ice by volume
4. Fill with cold water until the bags are approximately three-quarters full
5. Allow the mixture to sit for 10-15 minutes. This soaking period allows the ice to fully chill all material and begin the embrittlement process

### Step 4: Agitation

Agitation dislodges trichome heads from the plant surface. The technique and duration significantly affect hash quality.

| Agitation Method | Description | Quality Impact |
|-----------------|------------|---------------|
| **Hand stirring** | Gentle circular motion with a wooden spoon or dedicated tool | Best control; highest quality; most labor-intensive |
| **Drill mixer** | Paint mixer attachment on a low-speed drill | Consistent; less labor; harder to control intensity |
| **Washing machine** | Dedicated front-loader on gentle/soak cycle | Most consistent; least labor; requires dedicated equipment |

**General agitation guidelines:**

- **First wash:** 15-20 minutes of gentle agitation. The first wash typically yields the cleanest hash, as the outermost, most mature trichomes detach first.
- **Subsequent washes:** 10-15 minutes each. Each wash after the first yields progressively lower-quality material as more plant matter breaks down.
- **Number of washes:** 5-8 washes per batch of material is typical. Quality declines noticeably after wash 3 or 4.

**⚠️ Over-agitation is one of the most common beginner mistakes. Aggressive or prolonged agitation breaks plant matter into small particles that pass through the screens, contaminating the hash. Gentle, patient agitation produces cleaner results.**

:::

### Step 5: Settling and Collection

After agitation:

1. Allow the water to settle for 10-15 minutes. Trichomes sink through the bags while lighter plant material remains suspended or floats.
2. Starting from the bottom bag (smallest micron), carefully lift each bag and collect the hash that has accumulated on the bottom interior.
3. Use a dedicated collection tool (dab tool, spoon, or similar) to scrape hash from the bag surface.
4. Place collected hash on a clean surface for drying.
5. Label each collection by bag number -- different micron grades should never be mixed if quality assessment is desired.

### Step 6: Process Remaining Washes

After collecting from all bags:

1. Squeeze out excess water from the plant material (still in the 220 micron work bag)
2. Add more ice and fresh cold water
3. Repeat agitation (slightly shorter duration than the previous wash)
4. Collect and label as before

## Drying Ice-Water Hash

Proper drying is arguably the most critical factor in producing hash that can be preserved and used safely. Wet hash that is not dried thoroughly will develop mold within days, rendering it unusable.

### Cardboard Method (Traditional)

1. Press wet hash gently through a fine mesh screen (often the same bubble bag micron rating it was collected from) onto a piece of clean cardboard or parchment paper
2. This creates a thin, even layer that dries more uniformly
3. Place in a dark, dry environment with good air circulation
4. Ideal conditions: 60-70 degrees F (15-21 degrees C), 45-55% relative humidity
5. Drying time: 7-14 days depending on thickness and environmental conditions
6. Hash is fully dry when it crumbles rather than smears when pressed tip
Flip or break up the hash periodically during drying to expose all surfaces to air. Uneven drying can leave moist pockets where mold develops.
:::

### Freeze Drying (Professional)

Freeze drying (lyophilization) is the gold standard for ice-water hash drying:

1. Place wet hash directly into the freeze dryer
2. The process sublimates water directly from ice to vapor, bypassing the liquid phase
3. Drying time: 24-48 hours
4. Result: Exceptionally clean, dry hash with maximum terpene preservation

| Method | Time | Terpene Preservation | Quality | Cost |
|--------|------|---------------------|---------|------|
| Cardboard air dry | 7-14 days | Good | Good to very good | Very low |
| Freeze drying | 24-48 hours | Excellent | Excellent | $2,000-$5,000+ for equipment |

## Pressing Hash into Rosin

Ice-water hash can be pressed into rosin using heat and pressure, creating an even more refined and versatile concentrate. See [Rosin Press](/extraction/rosin-press) for detailed instructions.

**General guidelines for hash rosin:**
- Press temperature: 160-180 degrees F (71-82 degrees C)
- Use filter bags (37-90 micron) to contain the hash and filter plant matter
- Yields are typically 60-90% of the starting hash weight

## Quality Grading

Hash quality is primarily assessed by how it behaves when heated -- the "melt test."

### Melt Test Grades

| Grade | Description | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-------|------------|----------------|----------|
| **Full melt (6-star)** | Hash that melts completely with zero or near-zero residue | Clean bubbles, clear liquid residue, no plant matter | Dabbing; pressing into premium rosin |
| **Half melt (4-5 star)** | Hash that mostly melts with minimal residue | Some plant matter visible after melting | Dabbing; good-quality rosin |
| **Remelt (3 star)** | Hash that melts but leaves noticeable residue | Visible plant particles; some clean melt | Mixing with flower; lower-grade rosin |
| **Food grade (1-2 star)** | Significant plant matter; does not melt cleanly | Dark color, leafy texture | Edibles; mixing with flower |

### Star Rating System (Commonly Used)

| Stars | Bag Sources | Quality |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **6-star** | 45-73 micron, full melt only | Highest quality; dabbing grade |
| **5-star** | 45-73 micron, near-full melt | Very high quality |
| **4-star** | 73-120 micron, half melt | Good quality |
| **3-star** | Mixed bags, remelt | Moderate quality |
| **2-star** | 120-160+ micron, food grade | Lower quality; edibles |
| **1-star** | High plant contamination | Lowest quality; edibles only |

## Advantages of Ice-Water Hash

| Advantage | Description |
|-----------|------------|
| **Solventless** | No chemical solvents used or present in the final product |
| **Full-spectrum** | Preserves all cannabinoids and terpenes from the source material |
| **High quality** | Full-melt hash rivals solvent-based concentrates in purity |
| **Versatile** | Can be dabbed, pressed into rosin, added to edibles, or mixed with flower |
| **Scalable** | Works for small home batches or large-scale production |
| **Safe** | No fire, explosion, or chemical hazards |
| **Cost-effective** | Relatively inexpensive equipment compared to solvent-based methods |

## Disadvantages and Limitations

| Limitation | Description |
|-----------|------------|
| **Labor intensive** | Multiple washes, collection, and drying require significant time and effort |
| **Material loss** | Not all trichomes are recovered; some remain in the plant material after washing |
| **Water usage** | Each wash requires 5+ gallons of water and significant amounts of ice |
| **Drying time** | Air drying takes 1-2 weeks; freeze drying is expensive |
| **Yield variability** | Yields depend heavily on starting material quality, technique, and number of washes |
| **Plant contamination** | Some plant matter inevitably passes through screens, especially in later washes |

## Edible Applications

Ice-water hash is widely used in edible preparation, with lower-grade material (1-3 star) explicitly labeled as "edibles grade" or "food grade" throughout the hash community. This reflects a practical reality: material that is less ideal for dabbing due to plant matter contamination or chlorophyll content performs perfectly well -- and often excellently -- when used as an edible ingredient.

### Suitability for Edibles

Ice-water hash is **very well suited for edibles**, particularly the lower star ratings that are not economical or pleasant to dab. The star rating system directly maps to edible suitability:

| Star Rating | Edible Suitability | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| **1-2 star** | **Ideal for edibles** | Higher plant matter and chlorophyll content makes this grade unpalatable for dabbing, but these compounds are irrelevant in edibles where flavor is masked by other ingredients |
| **3 star** | **Good for edibles** | Moderate purity; fine for edibles, though dabbing-grade material may be better used for concentrates |
| **4-6 star** | **Premium edibles** | Full-melt hash can absolutely be used for edibles, but the cost-to-benefit ratio is poor -- these grades command premium prices for dabbing | tip
Using 1-2 star hash for edibles is an excellent way to extract maximum value from your ice-water hash production. The plant matter that makes it unsuitable for clean dabbing does not negatively impact edible experiences.
:::

### Why Lower-Grade Hash Works for Edibles

The compounds that make low-grade hash less ideal for dabbing -- chlorophyll, small plant particles, and darker color -- are largely irrelevant when the hash is decarboxylated and incorporated into food. The baking process and other ingredients mask the grassy flavor and visual appearance that would be undesirable in a dab.

### Decarboxylation Requirements

All ice-water hash contains cannabinoids primarily in their acidic forms (THCA, CBDA) and **must be decarboxylated** before use in edibles.

**General decarb guidance for ice-water hash:**
- Spread hash evenly on parchment-lined baking sheet
- Heat at 220-240 degrees F (104-116 degrees C) for 25-45 minutes
- Hash decarboxylates faster than flower due to concentration
- Lower-grade hash (higher plant matter content) may require slightly longer decarb times
- The hash will visibly bubble during decarboxylation as CO2 is released -- this is normal

Lower-grade hash may produce more visible plant material after decarboxylation, but this does not affect potency or safety. See [Decarboxylation](/science/decarboxylation) for complete guidance.

### Dosing Guidance

Ice-water hash potency varies by grade:

- **Full-melt (5-6 star):** Approximately 50-80% total cannabinoids
- **Mid-grade (3-4 star):** Approximately 30-60% total cannabinoids
- **Food grade (1-2 star):** Approximately 20-50% total cannabinoids (more plant matter dilutes potency)

**Practical dosing example:**
- 1 gram of 2-star hash at ~35% cannabinoids contains approximately 350mg total cannabinoids
- Assuming roughly 50% of cannabinoids are THC (after decarb), that is approximately 175mg THC
- This would yield approximately 17 servings at 10mg THC each

Always start with conservative doses when working with homemade hash of unknown potency.

### Advantages for Edible Use

- **Solventless:** No residual solvent concerns whatsoever
- **Value recovery:** Lower grades that are uneconomical for dabbing become valuable edible ingredients
- **Full-spectrum:** Even lower-grade hash retains the full cannabinoid profile
- **Easy to infuse:** Hash dissolves readily into warm butter or oil after decarboxylation
- **Scalable:** Works equally well for single-batch brownies and large-scale edible production

For detailed edible preparation techniques and dosing strategies, see [Edibles](/consumption/edibles).

## Storage

Properly dried and stored ice-water hash maintains quality for months to years:

- **Container:** Airtight glass jar
- **Temperature:** Cool (60-70 degrees F / 15-21 degrees C) or freezer for long-term storage
- **Light:** Dark; UV degrades cannabinoids
- **Humidity:** Low humidity environment (below 62% RH)
- **Freezer storage:** Allow container to reach room temperature before opening to prevent condensation
