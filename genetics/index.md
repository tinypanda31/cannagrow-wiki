# Genetics & Breeding

Cannabis is one of the most genetically diverse cultivated plants in the world. Understanding the genetic foundations of cannabis is essential for cultivators seeking consistent results, breeders developing new varieties, and consumers trying to understand why different plants produce different effects.

This section of CannaGrow covers the science of cannabis genetics, the art and methodology of breeding, the various types of seeds available, and the fundamental differences between autoflowering and photoperiod genetics.

---

## Why Genetics Matter

The genetics of a cannabis plant determine much of its potential:

| Factor | Genetic Influence | Environmental Influence |
|--------|:-----------------:|:-----------------------:|
| **Maximum THC/CBD potential** | Very High | Low |
| **Terpene profile** | Very High | Moderate |
| **Flowering time** | Very High | Low-Moderate |
| **Plant structure and height** | High | Moderate |
| **Disease/pest resistance** | Moderate-High | Moderate |
| **Actual yield achieved** | Moderate | Very High |
| **Final potency realized** | Moderate-High | High |

**Genetics set the ceiling; environment determines how close the plant gets to it.**

## Key Concepts at a Glance

### The Three Subspecies

| Subspecies | Origin | Typical Traits | Modern Role |
|------------|--------|---------------|-------------|
| ***C. sativa* subsp. *sativa*** | Equatorial regions worldwide | Tall, narrow leaves, long flowering, energizing terpene/cannabinoid profiles | Foundation of "sativa" breeding; Haze landraces |
| ***C. sativa* subsp. *indica*** | Hindu Kush mountain region (Afghanistan, Pakistan) | Short, broad leaves, fast flowering, relaxing profiles | Foundation of "indica" breeding; Kush landraces |
| ***C. sativa* subsp. *ruderalis*** | Central/Eastern Europe, Russia | Small, hardy, autoflowering, low THC | Source of autoflowering genetics; crossed with sativa/indica for auto strains |

::: info
**Modern taxonomy note:** Most botanists classify all cannabis as a single species (*Cannabis sativa* L.) with multiple subspecies. The indica/sativa distinction popular in consumer culture does **not** reliably predict effects — cannabinoid and terpene profiles are far more determinative. See [[/science/terpenes]] for details.
:::

### Genotype vs. Phenotype

- **Genotype** — The actual genetic code of a plant. Two seeds from the same parents share a genotype family but each has a unique genetic combination.
- **Phenotype** — How those genes express in the actual plant, influenced by both genetics AND environment. Two plants with nearly identical genotypes can show different phenotypes under different growing conditions.

**Example:** Two seeds from a Blue Dream cross may share the same parentage (genotype family) but one may express as more indica-dominant in structure while another expresses as more sativa-dominant. These are different **phenotypes** ("keeps" or "pheno-hunts").

### Breeding Generations

| Generation | Description | Traits |
|------------|-------------|--------|
| **P (Parent)** | The original parent plants | Known genetics, used as breeding foundation |
| **F1** | First filial generation — direct offspring of two distinct parents | Vigorous (hybrid vigor), uniform if parents are stable, but genetics not yet fixed |
| **F2** | Offspring of two F1 plants crossed together | Maximum genetic variation; wide range of phenotypes appears; pheno-hunting stage |
| **F3+** | Subsequent generations with selective breeding | Traits gradually stabilize; unwanted genetics bred out |
| **Stabilized Cultivar** | After 5-8+ generations of selective breeding | Traits reliably expressed in nearly all offspring; "true breeding" |

## Quick Reference: Common Breeding Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Landrace** | A strain that evolved naturally in a specific geographic region over centuries without intentional breeding |
| **Cultivar** | A cultivated variety produced through intentional breeding; short for "cultivated variety" |
| **Inbred Line (IBL)** | A line bred through multiple generations of sibling or self-pollination to fix traits |
| **Backcross (BC)** | Crossing offspring back to one parent to reinforce that parent's traits |
| **Pheno-hunt** | Growing many seeds from the same cross to find the individual expressing the most desirable traits |
| **Keeper** | A selected phenotype retained for further breeding or cloning due to superior traits |
| **Reverse breeding** | Creating feminized seeds by inducing a female plant to produce male pollen |
| **Rodelization** | A natural stress-induced hermaphroditism where a female plant produces male flowers late in flower |
