# Strain Database

A comprehensive reference of notable cannabis cultivars, organized by type, with detailed cannabinoid profiles, terpene data, reported effects, and cultivation notes.

**ℹ️ **Note on Classification** -- The traditional indica/sativa/hybrid classification is a cultural convention, not a scientific one. A plant's effects are determined by its **cannabinoid and terpene profile**, not by its growth morphology. "Indica" plants tend to be shorter and broader-leafed; "sativa" plants tend to be taller and narrower-leafed. However, these morphological differences do not reliably predict psychoactive effects. Always refer to lab-tested cannabinoid and terpene data rather than type labels when evaluating a strain.**

## How to Use Strain Data

### Understanding Classification Limitations

The indica/sativa/hybrid system originated from botanical descriptions of plant morphology (physical structure), not from pharmacological effects. Two plants both classified as "indica" can produce markedly different experiences depending on their individual chemovars -- the specific combination of cannabinoids and terpenes they contain.

When evaluating a strain, prioritize:

1. **Cannabinoid profile** -- THC, CBD, CBG, CBN, and minor cannabinoid percentages
2. **Terpene profile** -- Dominant and secondary terpenes with their concentrations
3. **Certificate of Analysis (COA)** -- Third-party lab test results for the specific batch
4. **Individual response** -- Effects vary by person, tolerance, dose, and consumption method

### Reading Certificates of Analysis (COAs)

A COA is a document from an independent laboratory that verifies the cannabinoid and contaminant profile of a cannabis sample. Key sections include:

| COA Section | What It Shows |
|-------------|---------------|
| **Potency Panel** | Percentages of THC, CBD, THCA, CBDA, CBG, CBN, and other cannabinoids |
| **Terpene Panel** | Concentrations of individual terpenes (myrcene, limonene, caryophyllene, etc.) |
| **Residual Solvents** | Remaining solvents from extraction (for concentrates) |
| **Microbial Testing** | Presence of mold, bacteria, or other contaminants |
| **Pesticide Screening** | Detection of agricultural chemical residues |
| **Heavy Metals** | Lead, cadmium, mercury, and arsenic levels |

**ℹ️ **Total THC calculation:** Total THC = THCA x 0.877 + THC. THCA (tetrahydrocannabinolic acid) is the non-psychoactive precursor that converts to THC through decarboxylation (heating). The 0.877 factor accounts for molecular weight loss during conversion.**

### Understanding Lab Testing Variability

Different laboratories may report different values for the same sample due to:

- **Sampling variation** -- Different parts of the same plant can have different cannabinoid concentrations
- **Testing methodology** -- HPLC vs. GC-MS can yield different results
- **Sample preparation** -- Drying, grinding, and extraction methods affect results
- **Laboratory calibration** -- Equipment calibration and quality control standards vary

For these reasons, the values on this wiki represent **typical ranges** observed across multiple tests, not exact figures for any specific sample.
